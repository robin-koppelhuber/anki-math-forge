The triage rail narrows a deck by tag and by grading; the shelf narrows the projects by tag. They are the same control over different vocabularies, and writing it twice is how one of them ends up without the chips. Nothing is spelled out until you click the box: on a deck with forty tags the written-out list was most of the rail's height, and the sections below it were off the bottom of the screen. What is on shows as a chip underneath, because a filter you cannot see is one you cannot turn off. `join` says what two chips mean together, and the word sits between them so the answer is on screen rather than in a docstring. A deck takes the **union**: groups that each narrowed the last meant a second choice nearly always emptied the deck, and "these two tags" is the question actually being asked of a mixed deck. The shelf takes the **intersection**, because there the tags are facets of one project, `cpp` and `paper`, so picking both means the paper about C++, not forty things about either. Every option is a real link the server built with the selection already toggled into it, so the script only ever shows and hides. With it doing nothing the control is still a working list of filters, one click deeper. `param` is the query parameter the selection lives in, `now` is its current value, and a row is `{key, label, count, field}` where `field` groups the list under subheadings. #} {% macro pick(id, rows, chips, param, now, here, filters, placeholder, join='or') %}
